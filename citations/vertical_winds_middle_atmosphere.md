# Vertical winds in the middle atmosphere: sources

Collected 2026-10-22 for the droplet settling model (`prototype/work-2026-10-21-droplet-demise/`, `MeanVerticalWind`
and `VERTICAL_WINDS.md`). Each entry says what was taken from it and whether the number was read in the source
itself or only in a search summary. Numbers marked "summary only" should be checked against the paper before they
are quoted in the thesis.

## Mesosphere: mean (residual-circulation) vertical wind

1. **Vincent, R. A. (2019).** Trends and Variability in Vertical Winds in the Southern Hemisphere Summer Polar
   Mesosphere and Lower Thermosphere. *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres*. DOI
   [10.1029/2019JD030735](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2019JD030735).
   Summer polar mesospheric upwelling of a few cm/s, with estimated peaks of 2-6 cm/s and year-to-year variability.
   Basis of the +/-5 cm/s bounding cases. (Summary only.)

2. **Chau, J. L., et al. (2019).** Can VHF radars at polar latitudes measure mean vertical winds in the presence
   of PMSE? *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics* 19, 4485. https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/19/4485/2019/
   Expected mean vertical winds of a few cm/s, below what radars can measure directly; reports a mean of about
   -0.17 m/s from MAARSY over 80-90 km in summer, attributed to measurement effects. (Summary only.)

3. **NASA Technical Reports Server 19860018257 (1986).** Connection between mesopause temperature, circulation and
   noctilucent clouds. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19860018257
   Upwelling of some cm/s at the polar summer mesopause supports the formation of polar mesospheric clouds by
   holding ice particles aloft. (Summary only.)

4. **Sato, K., Kinoshita, T., Okamoto, K. (2013).** *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences*.
   https://www-aos.eps.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~sato-lab/en/publications/publications/PDFs/SatoKinoshitaOkamoto-JAS2013.pdf
   Gravity waves drive the mesospheric residual circulation; Rossby waves drive the stratospheric one.
   (Summary only.)

## Mesosphere: gravity-wave (oscillating) vertical winds

5. **Vertical velocities associated with gravity waves measured in the mesosphere** (University of Bath research
   portal). https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/vertical-velocities-associated-with-gravity-waves-measured-in-the/
   EISCAT VHF radar: quasi-monochromatic gravity waves with vertical-wind amplitudes up to about 2.5 m/s near the
   mesopause. (Summary only.)

6. **COSPAR 2006 abstract A-00185**, Arecibo incoherent-scatter radar vertical winds.
   https://meetings.copernicus.org/www.cosis.net/abstracts/COSPAR2006/00185/COSPAR2006-A-00185.pdf
   Vertical wind accuracy about 1 m/s over 68-88 km; observed amplitudes of a few m/s, dominated by gravity waves.
   (Summary only.)

7. **Observations of polar mesospheric summer echoes resembling kilometer-scale varicose-mode flows.**
   *Annales Geophysicae* 42, 29 (2024). https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/42/29/2024/
   Rare localised vertical velocities up to 60 m/s; not representative of the background. (Summary only.)

## Stratosphere: Brewer-Dobson circulation

8. **Butchart, N. (2014).** The Brewer-Dobson circulation. *Reviews of Geophysics* 52, 157-184. DOI
   [10.1002/2013RG000448](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2013RG000448).
   Review: tropical ascent, poleward flow, descent at middle and high latitudes; poorly constrained by
   observations. Section 4.3 covers tropical upwelling; its magnitude was not read.

9. **The Brewer-Dobson circulation in CMIP6** (Deutsche Nationalbibliothek copy).
   https://d-nb.info/1230556524/34
   Models and reanalyses show a minimum in tropical upwelling of about 0.2 mm/s near 50 hPa. Probably Abalos et al.
   (2021), ACP; author list not confirmed. (Summary only.)

10. **Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics 26, 3743 (2026).**
    https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/26/3743/2026/acp-26-3743-2026.html
    The vertical branch of the circulation is around 0.1 mm/s, too small to measure directly; reanalyses disagree
    on tropical upwelling strength, especially below 70 hPa. (Summary only.)

11. **Seasonality of tropical ascent and extratropical descent**, *Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological
    Society*, DOI [10.1002/qj.966](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/qj.966).
    Strongest tropical ascent in each hemisphere's summer; strongest extratropical descent in the winter
    hemisphere. (Summary only.)

12. **Butchart, N., SPARC 2000 poster.**
    https://www.atmosp.physics.utoronto.ca/SPARC/SPARC2000_new/PosterSess3/Session3_4/Butchart/XABS/node3.html
    Climate-model strengthening of the circulation is mostly wave-driven. (Summary only.)

13. **Randel, W. J., et al. (2008).** *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences*.
    https://acomstaff.acom.ucar.edu/randel/JAS_2008.pdf
    Tropical upwelling and its drivers. (Not read; listed as a likely primary source.)

## Particle transport context and wind data

14. **Distribution of meteoric smoke - sensitivity to microphysical properties and atmospheric conditions.**
    *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics* 6, 4415 (2006). https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/6/4415/2006/
    In a one-dimensional model, the background vertical wind is one of the two most important factors for the size
    and number of nanometre smoke particles. (Summary only.)

15. **Detection and effects of meteoric smoke particles in the atmosphere** (review). https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.05223

16. **SD-WACCM climatology of mesosphere and lower-thermosphere residual circulations** (Zenodo, about 300 MB).
    https://zenodo.org/record/5804538 - a possible source of a latitude- and season-resolved vertical-wind profile.

17. **WACCM-X monthly means** (CEDA). https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/45283390b97c4a27861d74b3d915b0bd/
