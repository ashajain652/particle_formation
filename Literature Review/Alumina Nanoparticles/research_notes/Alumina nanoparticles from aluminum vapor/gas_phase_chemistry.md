# Gas-phase chemistry from aluminium vapour to the first condensable aluminium–oxygen clusters

Scope of these notes: the elementary gas-phase reactions that take aluminium atoms (Al) to aluminium suboxides (AlO, Al2O, AlO2, Al2O2 — "suboxides" meaning molecules with less oxygen per aluminium than alumina, Al2O3) and on to the first stable (Al2O3)n clusters; the rate constants and thermochemistry for those steps; why the step into condensed alumina is not a simple monomer-condensation process; equilibrium composition and condensation temperatures; and which published kinetic mechanisms a modeller could adopt. Rate constants are given in the units the source used: cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1 for two-body reactions and cm6 molecule^-2 s^-1 for three-body reactions (a three-body reaction "+ M" needs a collision partner M to carry away excess energy). Energies are in kJ/mol unless marked as kcal/mol (1 kcal = 4.184 kJ).

## 1. Key elementary reactions and recommended rate constants

### Takeaway
The first step, Al + O2 giving AlO + O, is fast (about 1.7 × 10^-10 cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1 at room temperature, close to the gas-kinetic collision rate) and almost thermoneutral, so Al vapour is converted to AlO within a few collision times wherever O2 is present. Beyond AlO, the routes to AlO2, Al2O, Al2O2 and the Al2O3 molecule are either endothermic (AlO + O2 giving AlO2 + O needs about 106 kJ/mol), or require a three-body collision (AlO + O + M, AlO + AlO + M), so the speed of the later steps depends strongly on gas density. The most complete, openly published, quantum-chemistry-based rate set is the network in Gobrecht et al. (2022), Table B.1. Combustion mechanisms differ from one another by more than five orders of magnitude in individual rate constants.

### Cited Findings

**Al + O2 giving AlO + O (the entry reaction)**
- The room-temperature rate constant measured with a laser-ablation fast-flow tube is k(298 K) = (1.68 ± 0.24) × 10^-10 cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1. The reaction enthalpy at 0 K is given from experiment as −14 ± 9 kJ/mol, meaning the reaction releases only a little heat. — [Gómez Martín et al. 2017, Chem. Phys. Lett. 675, 56](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/113322/)
- That 298 K value agrees with two earlier pulsed-laser-photolysis / laser-induced-fluorescence measurements (Garland & Nelson 1992 in a slow-flow reactor; Le Picard et al. 1997 in a CRESU supersonic-flow apparatus, 23 to 295 K), both giving 1.7 × 10^-10. The first temperature-dependent measurement by Fontijn and co-workers in a High-Temperature Fast-Flow Reactor (HTFFR, 300 to 1700 K) gave 3.2 × 10^-10 at 298 K, and Fontijn's group later agreed that value was in error. — [Gómez Martín et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/113322/)
- The upper-atmosphere aluminium model uses k = 1.7 × 10^-10 (T/300)^(1/6) cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1, i.e. essentially temperature-independent. — [Plane et al. 2021, JGR Space Physics 126, e2020JA028792, Table 1](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170006/)
- The astrophysical network uses k = 1.68 × 10^-10 (T/300)^-0.26 for the forward reaction and, for the reverse AlO + O giving Al + O2, k = 7.15 × 10^-12 exp(−945/T) from an RRKM fit (RRKM = Rice–Ramsperger–Kassel–Marcus statistical rate theory). Their high-level CBS-QB3 quantum calculation gives the reaction enthalpy as only −0.9 kJ/mol. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, A&A 658, A167, Table B.1 reactions 32–33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- A theoretical excitation-function study reported agreement with Garland's measurements over 298–1083 K and predicted that the rate constant falls at higher temperature, with a fitted form quoted in a search summary as 0.66 × 10^-10 T^(±0.2)/(1 + T/2100)^1.7. I could not open this source, and the sign of the temperature exponent in the summary is inconsistent with the room-temperature value (only a positive exponent reproduces about 1.7 × 10^-10 at 300 K), so treat this fit as unverified. — [Excitation function of the Al + O2 reaction (abstract record)](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/1363435)
- An early Air Force (Fontijn group) HTFFR report gives about (4 ± 2) × 10^-11 cm3/s at 1400 K; the scanned text is partly illegible, so the exponent is uncertain. — [DTIC AD0756644 / ADA028986](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA028986/index.html)

**Al + O + M giving AlO + M (direct recombination)**
- k = 3.34 × 10^-32 (T/300)^-1.79 cm6 molecule^-2 s^-1 (RRKM fit), releasing 498 kJ/mol; the reverse thermal dissociation of AlO is 4.01 × 10^-10 exp(−57391/T). — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Table B.1 reactions 26–27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- The AlO bond dissociation energy is 498–499 kJ/mol (CBS-QB3), consistent with standard bond-energy compilations. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)

**AlO + O2 (the second oxidation step)**
- The bimolecular channel AlO + O2 giving AlO2 + O is endothermic by 106 kJ/mol. Its rate is given, by detailed balance from the measured reverse reaction, as k = 3.54 × 10^-11 (T/300)^0.19 exp(−13140/T); the reverse AlO2 + O giving AlO + O2 is fast, 1.90 × 10^-10 (T/300)^0.17 (Mangan et al. 2020 RRKM fit). — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Table B.1 reactions 42–43](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- At low temperature AlO + O2 instead proceeds as a pressure-dependent association to OAlO2 (AlO3): log10(k) = −35.137 + 6.1052 log10(T) − 1.4089 (log10 T)^2 (three-body, with N2 as bath gas). — [Plane et al. 2021, Table 1, R6](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170006/)
- The association rate measured by Belyung & Fontijn (1995) over a wide temperature range was reproduced within a factor of 3 by an untuned master-equation (MESMER) calculation. — [Gómez Martín et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/113322/)

**AlO + O + M giving AlO2 + M**
- k = 6.74 × 10^-30 (T/300)^-2.53 cm6 molecule^-2 s^-1, releasing 391 kJ/mol; AlO2 thermally dissociates back with k = 4.51 × 10^-7 exp(−44166/T) (Plane et al. 2021 RRKM fit). The free energy of this reaction changes sign at about 2900 K, above which AlO2 is not favoured. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Table B.1 reactions 38–39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)

**Self-reactions of AlO and routes to Al2O and Al2O2**
- AlO + AlO can give Al2O + O (exothermic by 70 kJ/mol at 0 K, becoming unfavourable, i.e. its Gibbs free energy of reaction turning positive, around 1800 K), AlO2 + Al (suppressed by at least 100 kJ/mol at all temperatures), or Al2O2 through a three-body association that releases 548 kJ/mol. The Al2O + O channel is given the capture-limited rate 1.42 × 10^-9 (T/300)^0.17. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.2 and Table B.1 reaction 44](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- AlO's large dipole moment means its self-reaction could proceed near the gas-kinetic rate and may explain AlO loss in flow-tube experiments. — [Gómez Martín et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/113322/)
- The literature reactions that can make Al2O2 or AlO2 (the only two precursors of the Al2O3 molecule) are AlO + AlO + M, Al + AlO2 + M, Al2O + O + M, AlO + O + M, AlO + O2, AlO + CO2, and then Al2O2 + O + M and AlO2 + AlO + M to make Al2O3. Their rate constants mostly come from reversing the Catoire et al. (2003) / Washburn et al. (2008) mechanism by detailed balance, plus Sharipov et al. (2012). — [Boulangier et al. 2019, MNRAS, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09633)

**Formation of the Al2O3 molecule (monomer)**
- Oxidation of Al2O2 to Al2O3 is endothermic: by H2O, +85 kJ/mol (k = 1.78 × 10^-9 (T/300)^0.77 exp(−11797/T)); by OH, +28.2 kJ/mol (k = 2.57 × 10^-11 (T/300)^1.78 exp(−1977/T)), and the reverse Al2O3 + H is capture-limited and fast. AlO2 + AlOH giving Al2O3 + H is exothermic by 72.5 kJ/mol and remains so up to about 2700 K. Oxidation by atomic O is the most exothermic route but needs a third body for small molecules, so it only competes with two-body reactions at gas densities above about 10^14 cm^-3. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.2 and Table B.1 reactions 62–67](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)

**Hydrogen-bearing chemistry (humid air, combustion gas)**
- Al + H2O giving AlOH + H (−60.4 kJ/mol) has a two-term rate expression (Mangan et al. 2021) whose extracted text reads "1.7(-12) ... 422 ... T ... + 1.45(-10) exp(−2657/T)", i.e. a 1.7 × 10^-12 term with a 422 K temperature parameter plus 1.45 × 10^-10 exp(−2657/T); AlO + H2 giving AlOH + H (−45 kJ/mol) has k = 5.37 × 10^-13 (T/300)^2.77 exp(−2190/T); AlO + H2O giving AlOH + OH is slightly endothermic (+11.6 kJ/mol). AlOH + O giving products has a rate of about 3 × 10^-12 cm3/s between 1000 and 2000 K. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Table B.1 reactions 34–37 and Sect. 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139) (the exact grouping of the Al + H2O expression should be checked against the PDF table because the text extraction merged terms)
- In circumstellar (hydrogen-rich) gas the aluminium chemistry is controlled by AlOH and AlO, tightly coupled through AlO + H2, AlO + H2O and their reverses. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- In the upper atmosphere (80–110 km), AlOH is stable against H and O atoms and is likely a major aluminium reservoir; AlO, AlOH and Al+ are the three major aluminium species above 80 km. — [Plane et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170006/)

**Spread between mechanisms**
- A 2026 review comparing published Al gas-phase mechanisms (Storozhev, Glorian, Saba, Starik) finds that rate constants for the same reactions differ by more than five orders of magnitude, and that predicted droplet-flame temperatures differ by about 1000 K. The Storozhev mechanism reacts fastest, followed by Glorian, Saba and Starik. — [Qiu, Bai & Nilsson 2026, Appl. Energy Combust. Sci. 26, 100471 (record)](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/f1aeea3c-eee9-4bd2-9082-e20695f06c77)

### Inferences
- Using the Gobrecht/Mangan expression, AlO + O2 giving AlO2 + O at 3000 K is about 3.54 × 10^-11 × 10^0.19 × exp(−4.38), roughly 7 × 10^-13 cm3/s, which is about 250 times slower than Al + O2. AlO is therefore a bottleneck species in hot gas, which matches the strong AlO emission seen in aluminium flames.
- The three-body reactions (Al + O + M, AlO + O + M, AlO + AlO + M) only compete with two-body reactions above roughly 10^14 molecules cm^-3, per Gobrecht's estimate. The ambient number density at 60–80 km altitude is of order 10^15 to 10^16 cm^-3 (standard-atmosphere value, not taken from these sources), so in a reentry wake the three-body routes should be competitive, unlike in stellar winds. This makes the combustion mechanisms (which assume near-atmospheric pressure) more transferable to reentry than the astrophysical networks, but the pressure-dependent falloff of each three-body rate needs checking at the actual wake densities.
- For reentry in air, the H-bearing pathways (AlOH, OAlOH) matter only if water vapour or hydrogen from ablating materials is present; in dry air at high altitude the Al–O-only subset should dominate, with N2 acting as the third body.

### Gaps
- The original Arrhenius tables of Catoire et al. (2003), Washburn et al. (2008), Glorian et al. (2015, 2016), Starik and co-workers, and Saba (2021) were not accessible (paywalled, or the French HAL archive blocked automated download with a bot check). Values above therefore come mostly from the astrophysical and atmospheric networks, which use Mangan/Plane RRKM fits.
- I found no validated high-temperature (above 2000 K) measurement of Al + O2 or AlO + O2; the high-temperature behaviour rests on Fontijn's HTFFR data (partly retracted at 298 K) and theory.
- Huang/Yang-type nanoparticle kinetic models and a "Garland" high-temperature review were not located in open form.

## 2. Why there is no stable gaseous Al2O3, and how models bridge from suboxides to condensed alumina ("chemically driven nucleation")

### Takeaway
The Al2O3 molecule exists in quantum-chemistry calculations, but it is energetically disfavoured relative to the suboxides: forming it from Al2O or Al2O2 is endothermic with common oxidants, so very little ever exists in the gas, and condensed alumina cannot be modelled as classical condensation of a supersaturated Al2O3 vapour. Instead, current approaches treat particle birth as a sequence of chemical reactions — growth of (AlO)x clusters, which are unusually stable, followed by their oxidation, or reactions of suboxides directly on cluster or droplet surfaces — with Classical Nucleation Theory (CNT, the textbook model in which a supersaturated vapour forms droplets once they exceed a critical radius set by surface tension) failing badly for alumina.

### Cited Findings
- "A homogeneous nucleation scenario, where Al2O3 monomers are successively added, is energetically viable. However, the formation of the Al2O3 monomer itself represents an energetic bottleneck." Gobrecht and co-authors therefore suggest that efficient alumina formation either does not involve Al2O and Al2O2 or does not proceed via the monomer. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Gobrecht et al. find enhanced stability of (AlO)x clusters (x = 1–4, aluminium-to-oxygen ratio 1:1) compared with other stoichiometries, consistent with Patzer et al. (2005) and Lam et al. (2015). They propose growth by successive AlO addition (AlxOx + AlO + M giving Alx+1Ox+1, roughly 500 kJ/mol per new Al–O bond, needing a third body), or by AlOH addition (two-body, with H ejected; exothermic by 65.9, 36.0 and 76.0 kJ/mol for x = 1, 2, 3, and barely exothermic at x = 4). The oxidations of Al3O3 and Al4O4 to Al3O4 and Al4O5 are exothermic, while oxidation of Al3O4 is a further bottleneck. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Once the dimer Al4O6 forms, its association with another dimer to the tetramer Al8O12 (and the association of two monomers) releases more than 800 kJ/mol, with negligible reverse dissociation, which Gobrecht et al. use as the hand-off from explicit chemical kinetics to a thermodynamic treatment of larger clusters. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- When the stellar-wind network starts from an atomic gas, no Al2O3 clusters form at all: most aluminium stays atomic, with up to 1 % in Al-bearing molecules, and neither Al2O3 nor its precursors Al2O2 and AlO2 form anywhere in the temperature–density grid. The authors conclude that the literature rate constants for aluminium oxides "are incorrect and need revision or that alternative small Al2O3-cluster formation pathways are missing". When Al2O3 monomers are assumed present, however, (Al2O3)8 clusters form fastest and at the highest temperatures (1800–2400 K) of the four candidate dust species. — [Boulangier et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09633)
- Boulangier et al. show that treating cluster–cluster growth as rigid-sphere collisions overpredicts Al2O3 dimerisation by roughly an order of magnitude compared with the RRKM-plus-Lindemann rate of Sharipov & Loukhovitski (2018) at a total density of 10^18 m^-3; their plotted rate constants lie in the 10^-11 to 10^-9 cm3/s range over 500–3000 K. — [Boulangier et al. 2019, Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09633)
- CNT is especially poor for alumina: with a nanoparticle surface energy of about 1.5 J/m2 and a bulk free-energy change of about −62 kJ/cm3 at 298 K, CNT gives a critical radius of about one Bohr radius (0.05 nm), i.e. a single molecule, which is physically meaningless; CNT nucleation rates are generally off by tens of orders of magnitude. — [Lam et al. 2015 (arXiv 1707.08434)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08434)
- In aluminium-droplet combustion models, condensation is handled as a chemical step: Glorian et al. added two fast, irreversible global reactions converting gaseous Al2O3 (two isomers, "Al2O3" and "Al2O3c") into liquid Al2O3(L), with rate constants so high that they do not limit production; Muller et al. made them reversible so that liquid alumina can dissociate near 4000 K. The earlier Beckstead et al. (2005) model used a condensation-rate expression derived from nucleation theory, plus a dissociation threshold to cap the flame temperature. — [Muller, Davidenko & Giovangigli 2017, EUCASS2017-174](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Glorian et al. also developed a detailed surface ("heterogeneous") mechanism, with surface species and reactions on the droplet, showing that surface reactions of suboxides matter for aluminium combustion. — [Glorian, Gallier & Catoire 2016, Combust. Flame 168, 378 (record)](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/on-the-role-of-heterogeneous-reactions-in-aluminum-combustion/)
- Savel'ev & Starik (2018) model alumina nucleation in aluminised-fuel combustion as chemical reactions forming (Al2O3)n clusters up to n = 75, but only via monomer addition and using interpolated (not computed) Gibbs energies for n = 5–75; nucleation occurs on micro- to millisecond timescales because combustion gas is far denser than a stellar wind. — [described in Gobrecht et al. 2022 and Boulangier et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09633); original: Combust. Flame 196 (2018) 223, DOI 10.1016/j.combustflame.2018.06.017
- The upper-atmosphere model replaces nucleation entirely with a lumped "polymerisation" sink (AlX + AlY giving Al2XY, where X and Y are O, OH or (OH)2), whose rate constant is about 80 times a dipole–dipole capture rate to account for co-polymerisation with Fe, Mg and Na compounds. The value is given as 5.8 × 10^-8 cm3/s in the text and 5.8 × 10^-7 in Table 1 — an internal inconsistency in the paper. — [Plane et al. 2021, Sect. 2.5 and Table 1](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170006/)

### Inferences
- For the user's model, "chemically driven nucleation" in practice means one of three things: (a) a lumped fast sink from a gaseous oxide pseudo-species to condensed alumina (combustion style); (b) explicit cluster chemistry through (AlO)x, Al2O2 or AlO2, up to a stable seed such as Al4O6 or Al8O12, then collision-driven growth (astrophysics style); or (c) a polymerisation sink (atmospheric style). Option (b) is the most physically grounded but has the largest rate-constant uncertainty, and the stellar-wind work shows it can fail to produce any particles when starting from atoms.
- Because the true nucleation step is a chemical reaction rather than a supersaturation threshold, the number of particles formed is likely controlled by how quickly the gas cools through the 2000–3000 K window relative to the AlO and Al2O2 reaction times. The final particle size distribution will be very sensitive to the poorly known three-body and cluster-association rate constants.

### Gaps
- No experimental rate constant was found for any cluster-forming step (AlO + AlO + M, Al2O2 + O + M, cluster oxidation).
- Li & Truhlar and Sarou-Kanian were named in the brief, but I did not locate their alumina-cluster or alumina-vaporisation papers in open form in this session.

## 3. Quantum-chemistry and cluster studies: first stable clusters and binding energies (including the astrophysical alumina-dust literature)

### Takeaway
Calculations agree that small clusters with an Al:O ratio of 1:1 ((AlO)x) are the most stable at high temperature, that the Al2O3 monomer is a weakly bound triplet "kite" (about 388 kJ/mol per atom), and that binding energy per atom climbs steeply to the tetrahedral cage dimer Al4O6 (about 484 kJ/mol per atom) and approaches the bulk value roughly as n^-1/3. Clusters below about 4 nm are amorphous and not miniature corundum.

### Cited Findings
- Binding energies per atom (CBS-QB3 level, the most accurate method applied): AlO 249 (half the 498 kJ/mol bond energy); Al2O3 monomer 388 (triplet, flat, C2v kite shape; the linear singlet lies only 15 kJ/mol higher); (Al2O3)2 dimer 484 (cage of four six-membered rings with tetrahedral symmetry and all 12 Al–O bonds 1.744 Å long; next isomer 42 kJ/mol higher); (Al2O3)3 trimer 515 at CBS-QB3 (470 B3LYP, 476 PBE0). Density-functional values for n = 9–10 rise to about 514–521 kJ/mol per atom. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Other CBS-QB3 binding energies per atom: AlOH 327; OAlOH 352; AlO2 296 (the bent global minimum); Al2O2 386 (a diamond-shaped ring with no dipole moment, hence not observable by rotational spectroscopy); Al2O4 414; Al4O4 452 (dissociation energy 406 kJ/mol with respect to losing an AlO). — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.1 and Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- New global-minimum structures were reported for n = 7, 9 and 10; the fitted bulk limit, −1687.82 kJ/mol, agrees well with JANAF-NIST data for corundum. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Size-dependent structure: alumina is amorphous below n of about 800 (diameter below 4 nm), cubic gamma-alumina for n of 800–1900 (4–11.6 nm), and hexagonal corundum (alpha-alumina) only above about 11.6 nm (from calorimetry by McHale et al. 1997 and Tavakoli et al. 2013). — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, conclusions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Lam et al. computed stable structures for AlxOy with x, y up to 4, plus (AlO)n with n up to 8 and (Al2O3)n with n up to 4, at the MP2//B3LYP level; they found more stable isomers than earlier work for AlO4 and Al2O3. — [Lam et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08434)
- Patzer et al. showed the 1:1 stoichiometry is the most stable for small clusters (x, y below 4). — [as cited in Lam et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08434) and [Gobrecht et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Swihart & Catoire (2000) computed 298 K enthalpies of formation (kcal/mol, CBS-Q method, with experimental values in brackets): AlO +17.0 [+16 ± 2]; linear OAlO −15.9 [−20.6 ± 8]; AlOAl (Al2O) −38.7 [−34.7 ± 4]; planar Al2O2 −94.5 [−94.3 ± 8]; linear Al2O3 −130.0 and C2v Al2O3 −101.7 [no experimental value]; OAlOH −210.8 [the JANAF value of −110 ± 15 is a rough estimate needing major revision]. They expect accuracy of 1–2 kcal/mol generally, but somewhat worse for aluminium oxides because the CBS-Q and G2 methods disagree more for them. — [Swihart & Catoire 2000, Combust. Flame 121, 210](https://www.eng.buffalo.edu/~swihart/Reprints/Swihart_CombFlame_2000.pdf)
- Mass spectrometry over solid alumina (Farber, Srivastava & Uy 1972) found AlO, Al2O, AlO2 and Al2O2 at partial pressures of the same order as Al and O, and gave formation enthalpies of 17.0 ± 2.0 (AlO), −33.2 ± 3.0 (Al2O), −44.3 ± 2.0 (AlO2) and −113.6 ± 2.0 (Al2O2) kcal/mol. — [Farber et al. 1972 (record)](https://dspace.bits-pilani.ac.in/items/891ca999-1a01-44df-ac30-790690a1f400) — note that the AlO2 and Al2O2 values conflict with the Swihart & Catoire values above (−15.9 and −94.5) by about 28 and 19 kcal/mol.

### Inferences
- The jump in binding energy per atom from the monomer (388) to the dimer (484) is about 96 kJ/mol per atom, which is why Al4O6 is a natural "first stable nucleus": once formed, it does not fall apart at flame temperatures, whereas the monomer is fragile and readily reduced by H or reverts to suboxides.
- The disagreement in AlO2 and Al2O2 formation enthalpies (about 80–120 kJ/mol) is large enough to shift equilibrium suboxide fractions by orders of magnitude at 2000–3000 K; a modeller should state which thermochemistry set (JANAF, Swihart & Catoire, or Gobrecht CBS-QB3) is used.

### Gaps
- Gobrecht et al. (2022) Part II and later papers in the series (if published) were not checked.
- I did not find calculated sticking or accommodation coefficients for AlO or Al2O2 on alumina clusters.

## 4. Equilibrium chemistry, condensation (dew-point) temperature and alumina vaporisation

### Takeaway
At equilibrium in an Al–O gas at a few bar, Al, O, O2, AlO and Al2O dominate above about 5000 K; Al2O2 and Al3O3 then take over; and the alumina stoichiometry (Al2O3)n only exceeds the (AlO)n stoichiometry below about 2000 K, and only if there is a slight oxygen excess (Al/O below 2/3). Liquid alumina does not boil congruently; it decomposes into suboxides, Al and O, with an apparent volatilisation temperature at 1 atm of at least about 4000 K. Condensation temperatures from the astrophysics literature (for example, corundum at 1677 K) apply to hydrogen-dominated, very dilute gas at 10^-4 bar, not to an aluminium-rich wake in air.

### Cited Findings
- Equilibrium composition from first-principles thermochemistry at 4 bar and Al/O = 0.6: above about 5000 K the gas is mainly Al, O, O2, AlO and Al2O; as it cools, Al2O and O fall together, giving Al2O2 and Al3O3; Al2O3 "never seems to emerge"; the alumina stoichiometry (Al6O9, Al8O12) appears only below 2000 K. The 2:3 to 1:1 ratio at 1500 K depends drastically on Al/O near 2/3, so a small oxygen excess is required to reach the 2:3 stoichiometry. These conditions were fitted to time-resolved spectroscopy of a laser-ablation plasma from an Al2O3 target cooling at 25 K per microsecond, and the measured AlO/Al ratio was consistent with quasi-equilibrium because Al + O and Al + O2 have no energy barrier. — [Lam et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08434)
- In circumstellar (hydrogen-rich, low-pressure) equilibrium: aluminium is mostly Al+ above 2500 K, atomic Al at 2000–2500 K, and AlOH and Al2O below 2000 K; AlO peaks at a fraction of about 10^-8 near 2130 K; AlO2 is negligible; the alumina dimer only dominates below 800 K. Equilibrium therefore fails to predict observed alumina dust forming at 1.4–3 stellar radii, where it is much hotter. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- The 50 % condensation temperature of corundum (Al2O3) is 1677 K for solar-system composition and 1665 K for photospheric composition, at a total pressure of 10^-4 bar; condensation is defined as the point where the thermodynamic activity of Al2O3 reaches 1, computed from the equilibrium constant of 2Al(g) + 1.5 O2 giving Al2O3(s). — [Lodders 2003, ApJ 591, 1220, Table 7](https://www.ucolick.org/~woosley/ay220-19/papers/lodders03.pdf)
- A University of Illinois thesis estimated from extinction measurements that the volatilisation temperature of alumina at 1 atm is at least about 4000 K and does not limit micron-sized aluminium combustion; it notes earlier values relied on large extrapolations (seen only via search summary; the page returned an access error). — [Illinois IDEALS item 17098](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/17098)
- Muller et al. treat liquid-alumina dissociation as physically important "at a temperature approaching 4000 K". — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Single-particle aluminium flame temperatures plateau near 3800 K, and AlO emission thermometry gives about 3200 K for micron-scale aluminium (from search summaries of Penn State flame-structure studies). — [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/flames-structure-measurement-of-single-isolated-aluminum-particle/)
- Very-high-temperature mass spectrometry of Al2O3 and other refractory materials covers 3000–5000 K and 0.01–10 bar. — [Hastie, Bonnell & Schenck 2000, Pure Appl. Chem. 72, 2111 (NIST record)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/development-and-application-very-high-temperature-mass-spectrometry-vapor-pressure)
- Glassman's criterion says a metal burns in the vapour phase if the metal's boiling point is below the oxide's volatilisation temperature; Glassman later stressed that the oxide's "boiling point" need not be known to decide this. — [Glassman 1993, Combust. Flame 93 (NTRS record)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930054376)

### Inferences
- For a reentry wake, the relevant "dew point" is the temperature at which the product of the Al and O (or AlO and O2) partial pressures exceeds the equilibrium constant for forming liquid Al2O3. With aluminium mole fractions far above stellar values (Al/H about 3 × 10^-6 in Lodders' gas) and pressures of 10^-5 to 10^-2 bar, it will lie well above 1677 K. It should be computed directly with JANAF data (for example in NASA CEA or Cantera) for the actual Al/O ratio and pressure, rather than taken from either the astrophysical or the 1 atm combustion values.
- Because condensed alumina coexists with substantial suboxide vapour above about 3000 K, a model should allow condensation to be reversible above about 3000–4000 K, as Muller et al. did.

### Gaps
- I did not find an open, tabulated alumina dew-point curve as a function of pressure and Al/O ratio for air; this needs to be computed.
- An authoritative 1 atm boiling or decomposition temperature for Al2O3 (values of about 3250 to 4000 K are quoted in the brief) and the aluminium boiling point (about 2790 K is commonly quoted; one secondary source says 2730 K) were not verified against JANAF in this session.
- The Farber (1972) and Paule (1976) vapour-pressure equations for the Al–O system over alumina were not accessible.

## 5. Kinetic mechanisms a modeller could adopt

### Takeaway
There is no single validated mechanism. The practical choices are the compact Catoire–Swihart-derived combustion mechanism as used by Glorian (Al–O subset: 10 species and 15 reactions; Al–H–O: 20 and 44), the open astrophysical network of Gobrecht et al. 2022 (with RRKM rates and explicit clusters to Al8O12), the Starik group's mechanism (with (Al2O3)n cluster nucleation up to n = 75), Saba's TST/RRKM mechanism, or the 36-reaction upper-atmosphere scheme of Plane et al. 2021 for the cold, dilute end.

### Cited Findings
- Glorian's gas-phase mechanism, derived from Catoire and Swihart via Washburn et al. (2008), as used by Muller et al.: Al–O system 10 gas species and 15 reactions; Al–H–O 20 species and 44 reactions; Al–C–O 12 species and 19 reactions. The paired surface mechanism (Al–O) has 6 surface species, 2 bulk species and 25 heterogeneous reactions (38 for Al–H–O, 33 for Al–C–O). The full mechanism also contains N and Cl species. — [Muller et al. 2017, Table 1](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Washburn et al. (2008) replaced the global gas-phase reactions of Beckstead et al. (2005) with the detailed Catoire–Swihart mechanism. — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Gobrecht et al. (2022) built a network for Al-bearing molecules and AlxOy clusters (x = 1–5, y = 1–6) up to the dimer, which coagulates to the tetramer, with rates from CBS-QB3, RRKM/MESMER, recent experiments and capture theory (used for 46 exothermic reactions), all listed in Table B.1. It includes H, C, Si, Cl and F chemistry. — [Gobrecht et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139)
- Saba et al. (2021) computed TST/RRKM rates for forming alumina up to the tetramer, using O2 and O as oxidants only, in the high-pressure limit and without hydrogenated species. — [as described in Gobrecht et al. 2022, Sect. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11139). Saba's 2021 thesis built about 30 reaction diagrams with CBS-QB3 energies for O2, CO2 and H2O oxidants and matched experiments for Al + O2, AlO + O2, Al + CO2 and Al + H2O, stated to hold above about 1400 K. — [Toyohashi University of Technology thesis record](https://repo.lib.tut.ac.jp/records/2000304)
- Starik, Kuleshov, Sharipov, Titova & Tsai (2014) built an Al–H2O mechanism from potential-energy-surface quantum chemistry that reproduces ignition temperatures in Al–O2–Ar and Al–H2O systems. The Starik, Savel'ev & Titova (2015) review describes a gas-phase Al combustion mechanism for nano-aluminium in air and steam. — [NYCU record of Starik et al. 2014](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/24410); [mathnet record of the 2015 review](https://mathnet.ru/eng/fgv215)
- Plane et al. (2021) give 36 reactions (neutral, ion–molecule and photochemical) for aluminium at 80–110 km, with a polymerisation sink to meteoric smoke. — [Plane et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170006/)
- A 2024 single-particle study used the Burcat thermodynamic database with the Catoire mechanism. — [BIT record](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/exploring-the-combustion-mechanism-of-single-micron-sized-aluminu)

### Inferences
- A reasonable starting point for a reentry-wake model would be the Glorian/Catoire Al–O (and N2 as third body) subset for the hot gas, with Al + O2 and AlO + O2 rates replaced by the measured and RRKM values above. Alumina formation would be handled either by the Gobrecht (AlO)x cluster route, or by a lumped reversible sink to condensed Al2O3, with sensitivity runs across the five-orders-of-magnitude spread reported by Qiu et al. (2026).
- The Plane et al. scheme is directly relevant to the colder, more dilute tail of a reentry wake above about 80 km, and shares the Mangan/Plane rate constants with Gobrecht et al., so the two can be merged consistently.

### Gaps
- The species and reaction counts for the Starik, Storozhev and Saba mechanisms were not found in open sources.
- The Qiu et al. (2026) review would be the best single reference for choosing among mechanisms; its PDF could not be retrieved, though the journal is open access, so it may be downloadable by hand from the DOI.
