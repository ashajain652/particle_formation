# Alumina "smoke" nanoparticles from burning aluminum particles and droplets: physical picture, measured sizes, smoke-versus-residue split, and models

Scope note: these notes cover aluminum particle and droplet combustion as an analogue for alumina formed when molten aluminum droplets and aluminum vapor are shed into hot air during spacecraft reentry. "Smoke" (also "smoke oxide", "fine oxide", "SOP") means the fine aluminum oxide (Al2O3) that condenses from the gas phase in or near the flame. "Residue", "oxide cap" or "lobe" means the coarse oxide that collects on the burning droplet and is left behind when the aluminum is used up. Where a fact comes only from an abstract or a search snippet rather than from the full text I read, that is said in the bullet.

## 1. Flame structure of a burning aluminum droplet: flame standoff, flame temperature near the alumina dissociation limit, where condensation happens, and how suboxides and oxide reach the droplet surface

### Takeaway
Micron-sized and larger aluminum particles in oxygen-containing gases burn mainly in a detached vapor-phase diffusion flame whose diameter is about 1.5 to 5 times the particle diameter (smaller, about 1.3 to 1.5 times, in water vapor), with a peak temperature pinned near the alumina dissociation/volatilization limit of roughly 3600 to 4000 K depending on pressure and oxidizer. The suboxide AlO is the main gas-phase precursor of condensed alumina, nanoparticles nucleate in a zone roughly one particle radius away from the surface, and part of the condensed and gaseous oxide is carried back to the droplet where it builds the oxide cap.

### Cited Findings
- The adiabatic flame temperature of aluminum in pure oxygen at 1 atm is 3968 to 3977 K; it falls to 3546 K in air-like 21 % oxygen and to 3052 K in water vapor, and for most oxidizers stays below the alumina boiling (volatilization) temperature of about 4000 K. The fluorine system (about 4400 K) is the exception. The physical reason is that the heat released by aluminum oxidation is less than the energy needed to heat and vaporize the oxide, so the flame temperature is capped by oxide vaporization/dissociation. — [Sundaram, Yang & Yetter 2017, Prog. Energy Combust. Sci. 61:293-365, section 8.1.1](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- The boiling temperature of aluminum rises from 2373 K at 0.1 atm to 4441 K at 100 atm. Above a threshold pressure the flame temperature falls below the aluminum boiling point and surface reactions become more important; that threshold is about 2 atm for water, 5 atm for carbon dioxide and 100 atm for air. For water, the threshold drops from about 2.0 atm to 0.2 atm as particle size goes from 1 mm to 38 nm, partly because a 38 nm particle is 47 % oxide by mass. — [Sundaram et al. 2017](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- The flame standoff ratio (flame diameter divided by particle diameter, written D_f/D_p) observed for aluminum in oxygenated environments is 2.0 to 5.0, depending on oxidizer composition and pressure; in water vapor it is only about 1.3 to 1.5, and in carbon dioxide the radiation intensity falls with distance from the surface, both indicating a larger role for surface reactions. Measured flame temperatures of micron-sized and larger particles are close to adiabatic values. — [Sundaram et al. 2017, section 8.1.2](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- A continuum heat-balance model gives a flame-to-particle diameter ratio of 1.5 to 2.5, consistent with experiment. A free-molecular model predicts that the flame collapses onto the surface below a critical particle diameter of 6.1 (air), 7.2 (carbon dioxide) and 15.1 (water vapor) micrometres. (The extracted PDF text renders the unit as "mm" because of a font-encoding issue; the context of nano/micron particles indicates micrometres, but this should be checked against the figure.) — [Sundaram et al. 2017, citing their ref. 333](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- A summary used in a solid-rocket-motor model states that the flame radius of an aluminum particle burning in air is about twice the particle radius. — [Genot, Gallier & Schuller, EUCASS 2017-064](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-064.pdf)
- In a one-dimensional spherically symmetric model with detailed gas-phase and surface chemistry (ONERA), when liquid-alumina formation is made reversible (so liquid Al2O3 can dissociate back to gas), the peak flame temperature in oxygen/argon at 1 atm is bounded at about 3710 to 3740 K; in carbon dioxide it is bounded at about 3680 K at 1 atm and 3930 to 4040 K at 10 atm (depending on droplet diameter); in water vapor at 10 atm the "gaseous" regime peaks at about 3630 K. With irreversible condensation the peak exceeds 5000 K at 10 atm, which the authors call unphysical. The dissociation effect is confined to a zone within about six particle radii of the droplet centre. — [Muller, Davidenko & Giovangigli, EUCASS 2017-174](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- In the same model, droplets of 40 µm and larger burn in the diffusion-controlled regime (AlO mole fraction is zero at the droplet surface), and between 20 and 10 µm there is a clear transition towards kinetically controlled burning, with AlO appearing at the surface and the flame temperature and liquid-alumina fraction falling quickly. As diameter decreases, the temperature peak moves closer to the surface and heat losses grow. — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- In water vapor the ONERA model finds two coexisting regimes for the same conditions: a "gaseous" regime with a detached hot flame and a "surface" regime with a weak temperature peak right at the droplet surface and substantial surface oxidation. — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- High-speed quantitative laser absorption imaging of Al, AlO and AlH around a micron-sized droplet burning in steam (Lund, 2026) shows that the aluminum vapour concentration becomes steady about 13 ms after ignition and falls from high values at the surface to very low values several droplet radii away; hydrogen chemistry (AlH formation) dominates near the surface; and the AlO profile tracks the alumina condensate, identifying AlO as the main precursor of alumina. The vapour field is axisymmetric while the alumina cloud is asymmetric. (From the repository abstract only.) — [Wu et al. 2026, Proc. Combust. Inst. 42:106218, Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/0f1e4631-b60a-4337-a54a-1f59028fa03a)
- Single laser-ignited aluminum particles burned in O2/Ar, O2/N2, CO2, N2O and CO, with the condensed products' radial distribution mapped by electron-probe microanalysis, show envelope flames in O2, CO2 and N2O. The condensed-product envelope is largest in O2/Ar, then O2/N2, CO2 and N2O; in CO combustion appears to occur at or near the surface. Aluminum nitride or oxynitride is found near the surface in nitrogen-containing oxidizers (relevant to air). (Abstract only.) — [Bucher, Yetter, Dryer, Vicenzi, Parr & Hanson-Parr 1999, Combust. Flame 117:351-361](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/condensed-phase-species-distributions-about-al-particles-reacting/)
- In aluminum droplets burning in air, the zone where oxide nanoparticles form is separated from the particle surface by a distance about equal to the particle radius (that is, at about twice the particle radius from the centre, consistent with a standoff ratio near 2). (Abstract-level.) — [Karasev et al. 2006, Combust. Explos. Shock Waves 42(6), record](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/11587)
- Micro-arc experiments on aluminum particles in air (normal and microgravity) show three stages: a spherically symmetric flame at about 3000 K (stage I); then oscillating radiation, temperature near 2800 K and loss of symmetry of the flame and smoke cloud (stage II); then about 2300 K with oxide caps forming on the surface (stage III). Dissolved gases (oxygen, possibly nitrogen or hydrogen) are suggested as the cause of the switch to asymmetric burning. — [Dreizin 1999, NASA NTRS 19990054079 (abstract)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19990054079)
- The aluminum droplet first vaporizes, the vapour reacts in the gas phase, and gaseous products condense into liquid alumina; some alumina reaches the droplet surface (either by diffusion of gaseous products followed by surface condensation or by coalescence of alumina droplets with the aluminum droplet) and forms a growing lobe that makes the droplet non-spherical. The authors note many of these processes "remain unclear", so motor models use heuristics. — [Genot et al., EUCASS 2017-064](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-064.pdf)
- The oxide carried by a burning droplet is attributed to (a) the original oxide skin, (b) oxide formed while the particle sits on the propellant surface, (c) surface oxidation during burning and (d) diffusion of smoke oxide back to the surface; the relative importance is "not established". — [Dokhan, Price, Seitzman & Sigman 2002, AIAA 2002-4173](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/jpc20024173.pdf)
- A 2024 comparison of published aluminum gas-phase kinetic mechanisms (Storozhev, Glorian, Saba, Starik) found rate constants differing by more than five orders of magnitude and predicted temperature profiles differing by about 1000 K. (Abstract only.) — [Lund review of Al gas-phase kinetics in O2 and steam](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/f1aeea3c-eee9-4bd2-9082-e20695f06c77)

### Inferences
- For a reentry model in air, the relevant picture is a detached flame at roughly 2 particle radii from the centre, with a peak near 3500 to 3700 K at sub-atmospheric pressure, and alumina condensing in a thin shell at and just outside the flame. Because the aluminum boiling point falls with pressure (2373 K at 0.1 atm), low-pressure reentry conditions should favour vapour-phase burning even more than 1 atm laboratory air; the dissociation-limited flame temperature also falls with pressure, which shifts more of the oxide into gaseous suboxides that condense later and farther out.
- The finding that AlO is the main precursor (Wu et al. 2026) and that the pure (Al2O3)n stoichiometry only becomes favoured below about 2000 K (Lam et al., question 4) together suggest that condensation in the flame proceeds through suboxide clustering and is strongly temperature-limited near the flame peak; much of the condensation may occur on the cooler outer side of the flame.
- The large spread between published kinetic mechanisms (five orders of magnitude in rates, about 1000 K in temperature) means that a reentry model should treat the flame chemistry as a major source of uncertainty and test sensitivity to it.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full text of Glorian, Gallier & Catoire 2016 or of Bucher et al. 1996/1999, so I do not have their quantitative flame radii, condensed-oxide radial profiles, or the fraction of oxide returning to the surface.
- No source I read gives measured flame standoff ratios at sub-atmospheric pressures (below about 0.1 atm) relevant to reentry altitudes.

## 2. Measured sizes of smoke (fine oxide): primary nanoparticles, aggregates, log-normal parameters, morphology

### Takeaway
Fine oxide from aluminum flames consists of nanoscale spherical primary particles ("spherules") of about 5 to 150 nm, most often reported with means of 17 to 68 nm, which form fractal chain-like aggregates (fractal dimension about 1.6) of 0.1 to several micrometres that carry a few elementary charges; collected "smoke oxide" mass appears in a mode of about 0.3 to 2 µm because of this aggregation and coalescence. Spherule size grows weakly with the burning particle size (17 nm at 4 µm to 68 nm at 340 µm), and in-situ optical measurements show nanoparticles growing from about 40 to 110 nm along condensation trails.

### Cited Findings
- Russian (Novosibirsk) measurements over 1990-2010: alumina nanoparticles first form chain-branched aggregates with fractal dimension about 1.6, made of spherules 10 to 140 nm in diameter; aggregate outer size ranges from 0.1 µm to a few micrometres; most aggregates carry a charge of a few elementary units (positive or negative) and some are dipoles; Coulomb interaction explains the reduced fractal dimension (1.6 instead of the 1.8 expected for purely Brownian cluster aggregation). — [Zarko & Glotov 2013, Sci. Tech. Energetic Materials 74(6):139-143](https://www.jes.or.jp/mag/stem/Vol.74/documents/Vol.74,No.6,p.139-143.pdf)
- The mean spherule diameter increases from 17 nm to 68 nm when the burning aluminum particle diameter increases from 4 µm to 340 µm, i.e., a factor of 4 increase in primary size for an 85-fold increase in particle size. — [Zarko & Glotov 2013, citing Karasev et al. 2006 and Glotov et al. 2007](https://www.jes.or.jp/mag/stem/Vol.74/documents/Vol.74,No.6,p.139-143.pdf)
- The mass size distribution of condensed combustion products from aluminized propellants has several modes: mode 1, peaking at 0.3 to 2 µm, attributed to condensation of oxide vapour on nucleation centres followed by coalescence of the primary particles near the burning particle; mode 2, micron-sized oxide particles from the oxide cap left after full burnout (correlated with the parent particle size, e.g. a 4 µm mode from a 5 to 6 µm aluminum powder); and mode 3, agglomerates of aluminum plus oxide. The whole distribution spans about eight orders of magnitude in diameter. — [Zarko & Glotov 2013](https://www.jes.or.jp/mag/stem/Vol.74/documents/Vol.74,No.6,p.139-143.pdf)
- Burning aluminum and titanium droplets in air produce 1 to 10 µm fractal aggregates made of primary spherules of Al2O3/TiO2 5 to 150 nm in diameter (the range covers both metals). (Abstract-level.) — [Karasev et al. 2006, Combust. Explos. Shock Waves 42(6)](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/11587)
- The 2004 study "Formation of charged aggregates of Al2O3 nanoparticles by combustion of aluminum droplets in air" is the main primary paper on charge and aggregation; only the bibliographic record was accessible. — [Karasev, Onischuk et al. 2004, Combust. Flame 138:40-54 (NIST record)](https://firedoc.nist.gov/article/aXgzXYQBWEcjUZEYPReW)
- High-speed (200,000 frames per second) light-extinction and scattering imaging of condensation trails behind jetting aluminum droplets in aluminum dust flames shows nanoparticles appearing at the start of the trail near the droplet and growing from about 40 nm to 110 nm along the trail until they leave the Rayleigh scattering regime; nanoparticle formation accounts for about 50 % of the total alumina formation rate during the symmetric burning phase. (Abstract only.) — [Jüngst, Wu, Ruan, Aldén & Li 2025, Powder Technology 453:120633](https://finn.lub.lu.se/EdsRecord/edsswe,edsswe.oai.portal.research.lu.se.publications.b0089962.6cf0.49c3.9874.0ec6aed90152)
- Aluminum combustion in oxygen-containing environments "predominantly" produces oxide nanoparticles under 100 nm in diameter. A coagulation calculation for a 4 µm particle burning in air (2 ms burn time, 3100 K) gives a size distribution well fitted by a log-normal law; the coefficient of variation (standard deviation divided by mean diameter) is 39 % for an uncharged aerosol and 26 % for a thermally ionized (charged) aerosol after 2 ms. — [Poletaev 2025, Physics of Aerodisperse Systems 63:136-148](https://fas.onu.edu.ua/article/download/347248/342647/827223)
- Earlier modelling from the same Odesa group reported that the computed size distribution of aluminum oxide particles is only weakly affected by combustion temperature, pressure, oxidizer content and aluminum powder size. (Abstract only.) — [Karasevich et al. 2008, High Temperature (Teplofiz. Vys. Temp.)](https://mathnet.ru/eng/tvt1081)
- In propellant studies, "smoke oxide" is defined operationally as Al2O3 below about 1 µm (Dokhan et al. 2001) or "in the under 2 µm range" (Dokhan et al. 2002), separated from residual oxide by sedimentation; neither paper measured the smoke size distribution itself. — [Dokhan et al. 2001, AIAA 2001-3581](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/AIAA-2001-3581.pdf); [Dokhan et al. 2002, AIAA 2002-4173](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/jpc20024173.pdf)
- Traditionally, condensed combustion products are divided into smoke-oxide particles of characteristic size about 1 µm and agglomerates; fine oxide is "composed of nanosized spherules". (Search snippet.) — [Babuk et al., Politecnico di Milano / MATEC 2017](https://www.matec-conferences.org/10.1051/matecconf/201711001006)
- An older DTIC report found that the mass of fine oxide from two propellants corresponded directly to the amount of aluminum burned and reported smoke modes at 1, 3.5 and 7 µm, cautioning that these needed checking by independent methods. (Search snippet only; DTIC was under maintenance and the PDF could not be read.) — [DTIC ADA311150](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA311150.pdf)
- Burning nano-aluminum in air produced products that were not spherical: needle-like micron and submicron polycrystals, with substantial aluminum nitride from atmospheric nitrogen. (Search snippet.) — [EUCASS 2009 paper on nano-Al in air](https://www.eucass-proceedings.eu/articles/eucass/pdf/2009/01/eucass1p47.pdf)
- In combustion residues of micron-sized aluminum powders, the presence of nanometric spheres is taken as evidence of vapour-phase reaction, because alumina from vapour-phase reaction condenses as nanometric droplets. (Search snippet.) — [HAL hal-02018393](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02018393)

### Inferences
- For a reentry model, the two size scales should be kept separate: primary spherules (tens of nanometres, set by nucleation, surface growth and coalescence in the hot flame zone) and aggregates (0.1 to several micrometres, set by coagulation after the particles solidify). The commonly quoted "about 1 µm smoke" is an aggregate or sedimentation-defined size, not the primary particle size.
- The weak dependence of primary size on parent size (roughly diameter to the 0.3 power, from 17 nm at 4 µm to 68 nm at 340 µm) suggests that millimetre-scale reentry droplets would produce spherules of the order of 70 to 100 nm if conditions were otherwise similar; this is an extrapolation beyond the measured range.
- Because coagulation rate scales with number concentration squared, the strong dilution in a reentry wake should limit aggregation compared with dense propellant flames, so reentry smoke may stay closer to the primary-particle scale; this needs a wake-specific coagulation calculation.
- Flame ionization and particle charging (fractal dimension 1.6, a few elementary charges, narrower distributions in charged-aerosol models) may matter in a hypersonic wake, which is itself partially ionized.

### Gaps
- I found no measured geometric standard deviation (log-normal width) for spherule size distributions; only means and ranges, and the modelled coefficients of variation from Poletaev 2025.
- Primary-particle size measurements at sub-atmospheric pressure, or for millimetre-scale droplets in air, were not found.
- Glotov et al. and Karasev et al. full texts (Combustion, Explosion and Shock Waves; Combustion and Flame) were not open access.

## 3. Fraction of oxide going to fine smoke versus coarse residue or oxide cap, and its dependence on pressure, oxidizer and droplet size

### Takeaway
For single particles and propellant-burned aluminum, roughly 80 to 90 % of the oxide leaves as fine smoke and 10 to 20 % ends up in the residue/cap. For 100 µm particles, Glotov measured the deposited fraction at about 10 % of all oxide, independent of pressure between 7 and 80 atm; the deposited fraction falls as particle size decreases. Oxidizers that favour surface reaction (water vapour, carbon dioxide, and pressure above the aluminum boiling threshold) are expected to push more oxide onto the surface, but I found no quantitative split by oxidizer.

### Cited Findings
- Oxide smoke (diameters below about 1 µm) forms by condensation of gas-phase reaction products around a burning aluminum particle and "generally accounts for about 80-90 %" of the oxide formed; larger residual oxide forms from the original oxide skin plus oxide made by condensed-phase surface reactions during burning. — [Dokhan, Price, Sigman & Seitzman 2001, AIAA 2001-3581](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/AIAA-2001-3581.pdf)
- In AP/PBAN propellants with 18 % of 30 µm aluminum at 1.38 MPa (about 14 atm), the measured non-smoke residue was 15.9 % and 12.9 % of the expected total oxide mass (for 10 µm and 82.5 µm fine AP). The residue mass distribution has a peak near 20 µm, the expected size of the residue from a single 30 µm aluminum particle, plus a coarser tail from agglomerates; about half the aluminum burned as single particles and half as agglomerates. — [Dokhan et al. 2002, AIAA 2002-4173](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/jpc20024173.pdf); [Dokhan et al. 2001](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/AIAA-2001-3581.pdf)
- Replacing 20 % and 50 % of the 30 µm aluminum by 0.1 µm aluminum lowered the residue from 15.9 % to 8.3 % and 2.6 % of total oxide (10 µm AP), and from 12.9 % to 8.1 % and 1.3 % (82.5 µm AP); the reduction (85 to 90 %) is larger than the 50 % expected if the 0.1 µm aluminum simply burned to smoke, implying the nano-aluminum flame changes how the coarse aluminum burns. — [Dokhan et al. 2001, Fig. 10 and Table 4](https://seitzman.gatech.edu/AIAA-2001-3581.pdf)
- For monodisperse 100 µm burning aluminum particles in propellant combustion products, the final oxide particle mass is about 0.2 of the initial particle mass; after burnout, the oxide deposited on the particle is about 0.1 of the mass of all oxide formed, and this does not depend on pressure over 7 to 80 atm. The ratio of accumulated to carried-away oxide depends mainly on particle size: the smaller the particle, the less oxide is deposited and the more is carried away as smoke. — [Zarko & Glotov 2013, summarizing Glotov & Zhukov 2008](https://www.jes.or.jp/mag/stem/Vol.74/documents/Vol.74,No.6,p.139-143.pdf); [Glotov & Zhukov 2008, Combust. Explos. Shock Waves 44:662-670](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10573-008-0101-2)
- In aluminum dust flames, nanoparticle formation during the symmetric phase accounts for about 50 % of the total alumina formation rate, with droplet jetting (asymmetric phase leaving condensation trails) an important additional contributor; this is a formation-rate share, not a final mass split. (Abstract only.) — [Jüngst et al. 2025, Powder Technology 453:120633](https://finn.lub.lu.se/EdsRecord/edsswe,edsswe.oai.portal.research.lu.se.publications.b0089962.6cf0.49c3.9874.0ec6aed90152)
- Surface reactions are more prominent for aluminum in water vapour and carbon dioxide (lower standoff ratios of about 1.3 to 1.5 in water), and above a threshold pressure (about 2 atm in water, 5 atm in CO2, 100 atm in air) the flame temperature falls below the aluminum boiling point. — [Sundaram et al. 2017](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- The condensed-product envelope is largest in O2/Ar, then O2/N2, CO2, N2O, and combustion in CO is at or near the surface. (Abstract only.) — [Bucher et al. 1999](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/condensed-phase-species-distributions-about-al-particles-reacting/)
- Oxygen is about twice as effective an oxidizer as water and about five times as effective as carbon dioxide for burning rate; pressure and initial temperature have a minimal observed effect on burn time. (Abstract/snippet only; DTIC PDF unavailable at time of research.) — [Beckstead, RTO-EN-023 "A Summary of Aluminum Combustion"](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA425147.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible starting value for a reentry model is that 80 to 90 % of the oxidized aluminum becomes smoke and 10 to 20 % stays on the droplet, with the residue share increasing with droplet size. The 10 % figure from Glotov is for 100 µm particles in propellant gases at 7 to 80 atm; reentry droplets (often hundreds of micrometres to millimetres) in low-pressure air may deposit a larger share, but no data cover that case.
- Air at low pressure is the most vapour-phase-favourable combination in the Sundaram threshold analysis (air threshold about 100 atm versus 2 to 5 atm for H2O and CO2), so a reentry case should sit at the high-smoke end of the range, other things equal.
- Note that these fractions refer to aluminum that actually burns; in reentry, aluminum vapour shed into the wake without a sustained envelope flame is a different situation, and the fraction that condenses may be governed by wake cooling and dilution rather than flame structure.

### Gaps
- No quantitative smoke/residue split by oxidizer (O2 versus H2O versus CO2 versus air) was found in accessible full texts.
- No data at sub-atmospheric pressure or for millimetre-scale droplets.
- The fraction of smoke that diffuses back onto the droplet (versus surface oxidation) is not established; Dokhan et al. 2002 explicitly say so, and the Beckstead/Liang deposition submodel's fraction was not accessible.

## 4. Models of condensation in aluminum flames and their nucleation treatment

### Takeaway
Most single-droplet combustion models (Beckstead/Liang, Washburn, Glorian, ONERA/Muller) do not resolve particle sizes: they treat condensation as a fast or nucleation-derived global rate that converts gaseous Al2O3 (and sometimes AlO) into "liquid alumina" treated as a species, with a dissociation limit capping temperature. Size-resolving work is recent: population-balance models with nucleation, growth and coagulation (Finke & Sewerin 2023, 2024), classical-nucleation-theory Euler-Lagrange models predicting bimodal smoke (Fuel 2025), Starik's kinetic plus nonstationary nucleation model, sectional coagulation models with charging (Poletaev 2025), and first-principles cluster thermochemistry showing classical nucleation theory fails for alumina (Lam et al.).

### Cited Findings
- Beckstead et al.'s two-dimensional, unsteady model used global reactions for gas-phase and surface chemistry, heterogeneous reactions for evaporation, surface oxidation and condensation/dissociation of liquid alumina; the alumina condensation rate was derived from nucleation theory; a dissociation threshold limited the liquid-alumina fraction and hence the flame temperature; a particular Schmidt number described liquid-alumina diffusion; the oxide cap was a segment of the sphere that reduced reactive surface. — [Muller et al. 2017 summarizing Beckstead, Liang & Puduppakkam 2005](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf); [Beckstead, Liang & Puduppakkam 2005, Combust. Explos. Shock Waves 41:622-638](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10573-005-0077-0)
- The Beckstead/Liang/Puduppakkam model includes a submodel for condensation of the oxide product and deposition of part of it onto the particle; it predicts the burning-time exponent on diameter changing from about 1.2 for small particles to about 1.9 for large ones, and oxide deposition causing particle velocity oscillations. (Abstract only.) — [mathnet record](https://m.mathnet.ru/eng/fgv1728)
- Washburn et al. (2008) replaced the global gas-phase reactions with the detailed Catoire-Swihart mechanism but modelled only aluminum evaporation at the surface. Glorian et al. (2016) used the detailed mechanism in a two-dimensional axisymmetric code (CPS) around a constant-diameter droplet without oxide cap, added two global irreversible reactions Al2O3 to Al2O3(L) and Al2O3c to Al2O3(L) with very fast rates, and treated liquid alumina as an ideal gas in the equation of state and transport model. — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Making those condensation reactions reversible (allowing dissociation of liquid alumina) is "very important" in O2/Ar and CO2: it bounds the flame temperature near the dissociation limit and raises AlO in the flame; with irreversible condensation the flame can exceed 5000 K at 10 atm. — [Muller et al. 2017](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2017-174.pdf)
- Finke & Sewerin (2023) combined a population balance for the oxide droplet size distribution with detailed species and enthalpy balances in a perfectly stirred reactor; nucleation, growth and coagulation rates were derived from "low-level theories of phase transition and aerosol transport", and droplet dissociation and radiation were included. (Abstract/snippet only.) — [Finke & Sewerin 2023, Combust. Flame 248:112510](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010218022005193)
- A follow-up applies the population balance approach (nucleation, growth, coagulation) to predict the oxide smoke size distribution near a single burning aluminum particle, reported as Combust. Flame 265:113464 (2024). (Bibliographic details from a search snippet; not verified.) — [Finke, OvGU Magdeburg profile](https://www.mvt.ovgu.de/-p-258.html)
- A direct numerical simulation of a single burning aluminum particle with detailed gas and surface kinetics found a thin condensation layer at a stagnation plane that releases a large share of the heat, a feature not predicted by previous models, which mostly used homogeneous nucleation or nucleation plus surface growth. (Search snippet; paper not identified.) — search result, source unconfirmed; treat as unverified.
- A 2025 Fuel study built self-nucleation (classical nucleation theory), surface growth and agglomeration models coupled to an Euler-Lagrange solver (OpenFOAM) and found that the oxidized smoke particles have a bimodal size distribution whether or not agglomeration is included. (Abstract/snippet only.) — [Fuel 2025, "Numerical investigation of oxidized smoke particles from aluminum combustion products"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016236125031916)
- Starik, Savel'ev & Titova (2015) review aluminum nanoparticle combustion, present a gas-phase kinetic mechanism for aluminum, and describe a method for simulating formation of the Al2O3 condensed phase including nonstationary nucleation. (Abstract only.) — [Starik, Savel'ev & Titova 2015, Combust. Explos. Shock Waves 51:197-222](https://mathnet.ru/eng/fgv215)
- Babuk & Budnyi model the evolution of smoke oxide particles in the combustion-product flow, with condensation of aluminum oxides on existing smoke particles (heterogeneous condensation, the smoke acting as nuclei) and coagulation decreasing dispersity. (Snippet.) — [Babuk & Budnyi 2017, MATEC Web Conf. 110:01006](https://www.matec-conferences.org/10.1051/matecconf/201711001006); [Chemical Physics and Mesoscopics record](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/chphm318)
- Poletaev (2025) models free-molecular coagulation of Al2O3 nanoparticles in aluminum dust flames with a sectional Smoluchowski equation, particle charging by the orbital-motion-limited approximation, and Saha ionization equilibrium; charge narrows the distribution and weakens the growth of mean size with time; adding potassium (5×10^19 to 5×10^22 atoms per cubic metre) reduces the mean diameter from 72 nm to 9.3 nm for 5 µm particles at 3200 K and narrows the coefficient of variation from 31 % to 18 %. Nearly all particles fall in 8 to 10 size sections. — [Poletaev 2025](https://fas.onu.edu.ua/article/download/347248/342647/827223)
- First-principles calculations of AlxOy cluster structures and thermochemistry argue that classical nucleation theory fails for alumina because the seeds' structure and stoichiometry differ from the bulk. In equilibrium at 4 bar and an Al:O ratio of 0.6, the gas above about 5000 K is Al, O, O2, AlO and Al2O; on cooling Al2O2 and Al3O3 appear; the bulk (2:3) stoichiometry (Al6O9, Al8O12) only exceeds the (1:1) family below about 2000 K, and a slight oxygen excess is needed. Kinetics are not addressed. — [Lam, Amans, Dujardin, Ledoux & Allouche, arXiv:1707.08434 (published J. Phys. Chem. A, 2015)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08434)
- A first-principles approach combined with the gas composition evolution reproduces time-resolved spectroscopy of a laser-induced plasma from an Al2O3 target, so the cluster thermochemistry has been validated against an experiment in which alumina nanoparticles nucleate from a cooling Al-O vapour. — [Lam et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08434)
- Huang et al. model aluminum dust flames with a burning rate as a function of size and ambient temperature and find a transition from diffusion-controlled to kinetically controlled combustion as particle size decreases into the nano range; flame speed scales as d^-0.92 for micron particles and d^-0.52 or d^-0.13 for nano particles depending on the assumed burning-time law. (No smoke size treatment.) — [Huang, Risha, Yang & Yetter 2009, Combust. Flame 156:5-13](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/C&F%20(2009,%20Huang).pdf)

### Inferences
- For the reentry model, the most transferable framework is the Finke & Sewerin type: detailed Al/O/N gas chemistry plus a population balance (sectional or moment method) for oxide droplets with nucleation, surface growth (heterogeneous condensation of AlO/Al2O and Al2O3 vapour), coagulation and dissociation. Classical nucleation theory with bulk surface tension is likely to be wrong for alumina (Lam et al.); a chemically driven nucleation step through (AlO)n clusters is better supported, though no combustion model I found implements one in full.
- The reversible-condensation finding (Muller et al.) matters directly for vapour condensing in a hot wake: an irreversible "instant condensation" sink will over-predict temperature and under-predict AlO, and therefore misplace where and how much condenses.
- Primary-particle size is set mainly by the competition of nucleation, condensation and coalescence in the hot zone, while aggregate size is set by coagulation afterwards; a two-stage model (sintering/coalescence time versus collision time) is the standard aerosol approach and fits the observed spherule-plus-fractal-aggregate morphology.

## 5. Nano-aluminum combustion, aluminum dust flames, solid rocket motor smoke, and laboratory nano-alumina from aluminum combustion

### Takeaway
Nano-aluminum burns closer to the surface and colder than micron aluminum (flame temperatures well below adiabatic, a transition to kinetically controlled burning), and its products can include non-spherical polycrystals and nitrides in air. Aluminum dust flames produce sub-100 nm oxide whose size distribution is shaped by coagulation and flame ionization. Solid rocket motor exhaust alumina is bimodal or trimodal, with a fine smoke mode at or below about 1 to 2 µm and coarse modes set by residues and agglomerates; stratospheric plume sampling found alumina down to a few nanometres.

### Cited Findings
- For nano-sized aluminum, heat loss to the gas competes with heat release because of high surface-to-volume ratio, so particle temperatures are likely below adiabatic; measured flame temperatures of nano-aluminum at 4 to 8 atm are substantially below adiabatic and reach about 3400 K at 32 atm, where the bulk aluminum boiling point is about 3800 K, so surface reactions are expected; heat and mass transfer are in the free-molecular regime. — [Sundaram et al. 2017, section 8](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)
- In aluminum dust flames, flame speed rises and combustion moves from diffusion-controlled to kinetically controlled as particle size decreases from micron to nano. — [Huang et al. 2009](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/C&F%20(2009,%20Huang).pdf)
- In dust flames of micron aluminum, oxide nanoparticles under 100 nm dominate; thermal ionization and alkali additives can reduce mean size to about 5 to 10 nm and give nearly monodisperse distributions; potassium carbonate additive experiments show a minimum in characteristic size versus potassium concentration. — [Poletaev 2025](https://fas.onu.edu.ua/article/download/347248/342647/827223)
- Molten aluminum sprays (mean droplet size about 180 µm) produced by gas atomization and ignited in the post-flame gas of a fuel-rich hydrogen/oxygen flame above 2700 K show an alumina-encapsulated core-shell structure, aluminum vapour ejection, and droplet coalescence and breakup. This is the closest laboratory analogue to molten droplets shed into hot gas. (Abstract only.) — [Ni et al. 2026, Proc. Combust. Inst. 42:106235, Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/63218dd1-cd63-4785-bb1f-706b115fbd47)
- In aluminized solid propellants, smoke oxide forms both from non-agglomerated metal burning in the gas-phase layer above the surface and from agglomerating metal in the surface layer. (Snippet.) — [Babuk 2007, Fiz. Goreniya Vzryva (mathnet fgv1462)](https://mathnet.ru/eng/fgv1462)
- Beckstead's summary reports a bimodal Al2O3 distribution in small-motor exhaust, with most oxide as smoke of about 1.5 to 2 µm and a second fraction near 6 µm. (Snippet only; DTIC unavailable.) — [Beckstead, RTO-EN-023](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA425147.pdf)
- A NASA review states that earlier high-altitude sampling and static collection found main-burn Shuttle plumes dust-like, with typical particles around 5 µm and aggregates up to about 35 µm. (Snippet.) — [NASA NTRS 20070013536](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20070013536/downloads/20070013536.pdf)
- In-situ sampling in the stratospheric plume of an Athena II rocket measured alumina particles from about 0.004 to 1.2 µm. (Snippet.) — [Size-resolved particle emission indices in the stratospheric plume of an Athena II rocket](https://impacts.ucar.edu/en/publications/size-resolved-particle-emission-indices-in-the-stratospheric-plum/)
- Multi-wavelength transmission at the edge of a small solid-motor plume inferred a log-normal distribution dominated by submicrometre particles. (Snippet.) — [Measurement of submicrometer Al2O3 particles in plumes, Naval Postgraduate School](https://calhoun.nps.edu/entities/publication/d3a8c5f1-e4f3-4535-b7e9-9d3b03f58887)
- A TRW atmospheric model used a tri-modal initial size distribution for solid-motor alumina. (Snippet.) — [globalsecurity.org copy](https://www.globalsecurity.org/space/library/report/enviro/gsisrml.pdf)
- Laboratory nano-alumina synthesis routes include vaporization-condensation and combustion flame chemical vapour condensation; vaporization-condensation gives homogeneous nucleation of the solid phase from supersaturated vapour, and agglomeration is a drawback. — [Sundaram et al. 2017, section 2.2](https://www.yang.gatech.edu/publications/Journal/PECS%20(Sundaram,%202017)%20nano%20particles.pdf)

### Inferences
- Solid rocket motor "smoke" sizes (about 0.1 to 2 µm) mostly reflect aggregated or coalesced primary particles in a dense, high-pressure chamber and nozzle; they are an upper bound for what dilute reentry wakes would produce, while stratospheric plume data down to a few nanometres show that a nanoscale population survives in rocket plumes.
- The Lund molten-spray work (2026) is the most direct experimental analogue to reentry droplet shedding and is worth tracking for future size measurements.

### Gaps
- No open-access data directly measuring the alumina nanoparticle size distribution produced by laboratory combustion of aluminum in air for powder production were found.
- Goroshin's and Bidabadi's dust-flame papers were not reached; no specific numbers from them are recorded.
